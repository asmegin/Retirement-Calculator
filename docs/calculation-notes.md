# Retirement planning and calculation checks

The dashboard's **When can we retire?** card searches whole retirement years from the current age through 75, stopping before the configured plan horizon. It chooses the earliest year by which everyone can retire, then the fewest combined years worked. Moving either slider fixes that person's age and searches the other person's ages. **Find ages for both** clears that choice. **Use these retirement ages** applies and saves the displayed recommendation. Past retirement dates stay fixed. An unsuccessful search reports its age limit instead of inventing a retirement date.

The search checks every earlier candidate rather than assuming later retirement always improves funding. It uses the same tax, withdrawal, contribution, pension and spending-phase simulation as the dashboard. Work runs in a cancellable Web Worker; new settings discard stale results. These are projections under the chosen market assumption, not Monte Carlo success guarantees. Configured contribution targets must be affordable. The model ends when the younger person reaches the horizon and does not model a spouse's death.

Pensions are optional under **Configure → Pensions** and in the setup wizard. Add a plan name, member, lifetime monthly amount, bridge, cutoff and indexing. Optional age-specific estimates interpolate between entered ages and use the nearest amount beyond the range. Link commencement to retirement if the retirement search should also change pension commencement. Without age-specific estimates, the pension amount itself does not change with retirement age. HOOPP and older “Other pension” entries migrate once into named plans, preserving income and indexing, with their old sources cleared to avoid double counting. New plans do not receive an automatic HOOPP pension.

Every visible input and select in configuration, onboarding and the dashboard has a mouse, keyboard and touch-accessible help control.

## Tax and benefit scenarios

Tests use an explicit 2026 start year and zero inflation so the application's 2025 threshold base stays fixed. The application projects those thresholds using the user's inflation assumption; it uses the 14% federal first bracket for future years. It is not a historical 2025 tax-return calculator (the annual 2025 first rate was 14.5%). [Federal rate change](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2025/05/delivering-a-middle-class-tax-cut.html)

| Scenario | Implemented behavior |
| --- | --- |
| DB pension at 58, RRIF at 62 | DB income can split federally before 65. RRIF income starts qualifying at 65, including discretionary RRIF draws. Ordinary RRSP withdrawals do not qualify simply because the owner turns 65. An under-65 recipient does not get the federal pension credit for transferred RRIF income. |
| Eligible dividends and OAS | $75,000 of eligible dividends contributes $103,500 to taxable income before OAS and other income. Both non-registered eligible distributions and corporate withdrawals receive the gross-up and dividend credits. OAS recovery uses the grossed-up income. Non-registered distributions are reinvested, not counted as spendable cash. |
| TFSA and GIS | TFSA withdrawals do not enter taxable or GIS income. With $400/month CPP, full OAS and $500,000 TFSA, a $4,000 monthly spending goal retains approximately $865/month GIS in the current annual estimate. CPP reduces GIS; this is not the zero-income maximum. |
| Childcare | The $140,000/$30,000 couple with children born in 2019 and 2022 claims $13,000 in 2026 through Person 2: $5,000 plus $8,000, below the $20,000 earned-income cap. With Person 2 earning $12,000, the deduction falls to $8,000. |
| Rental sale | $500,000 building cost, $420,000 opening UCC, and a $750,000 sale with no costs produces $80,000 ordinary-income recapture plus $125,000 taxable capital gain. Historical CCA is included even with future CCA disabled. Selling for $350,000 instead gives a $70,000 terminal loss, with no building capital loss. |
| Quebec | Separate provincial brackets and BPA; 16.5% federal abatement; provincial splitting only when the transferring pensioner is at least 65. Schedule B combines spouses' age/pension amounts, applies the pension-income multiplier of 1.25 and a single 18.75% family-income reduction, then allocates the remaining credit. Federal and provincial tax and split amounts are visible in year details. |
| Ontario | Both the 20% and 36% surtaxes stack above $5,710/$7,307 in the threshold base. The health premium is $750 at $120,000 income and reaches $900 at $201,200, not at $120,000. Health-premium thresholds are not indexed. |
| RRIF conversion | Convert by December 31 of the year turning 71. Required withdrawals start in the following year and use age at January 1: 5.28% in the year turning 72, then 5.40% in the year turning 73. Earlier conversion can be set under People. Surplus after tax goes to available TFSA room, then non-registered savings when reinvestment is enabled. |
| HBP shortfall | Required repayment and designated repayment budget are separate inputs. A $1,516 requirement with zero budget adds $1,516 RRSP income on line 12900, which contributes to total income; it is not employment pay, new cash, or a reduction of RRSP room. Partial repayments include only the shortfall. Each scheduled year passes whether repaid or included in income. |
| Age amount | At 65, $42,000 income retains the full federal and Ontario age amounts. At $75,000, federal age amount is reduced by 15% of income above $45,522; Ontario uses its own $46,330 threshold. Quebec uses its separate family-income rules. |

Pension rules: [CRA eligible pension income](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/pension-income-splitting.html), [Quebec pension transfer](https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/citizens/income-tax-return/completing-your-income-tax-return/how-to-complete-your-income-tax-return/line-by-line-help/201-to-260-net-income/line-245/).

OAS and GIS: [OAS recovery](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/old-age-security/recovery-tax.html), [July–September 2026 benefit amounts and income assessments](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/old-age-security/payments.html). GIS remains an annual prior-income planning approximation rather than a quarterly benefit adjudication; partial OAS and Allowance cases are not modelled.

Childcare and rental taxation: [CRA childcare folio](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/technical-information/income-tax/income-tax-folios-index/series-1-individuals/folio-3-family-unit-issues/income-tax-folio-s1-f3-c1-child-care-expense-deduction.html), [CRA rental-income guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4036/rental-income.html). Rental sales treat each property's building as a separate pool emptied by the sale. Enter building cost excluding land and a supportable building share of sale proceeds; if omitted, the simplified assumption is that all cost and proceeds belong to the building. Land gains can reduce terminal losses. Capital losses are reported separately rather than carried across years. Childcare exceptions for disability, study, separation and other special cases are not modelled.

Quebec and Ontario: [Quebec Schedule B](https://www.revenuquebec.ca/documents/en/formulaires/tp/2025-12/TP-1.D.B-V%282025-12%29.pdf), [CRA tax packages and provincial worksheets](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/tax-packages-years/general-income-tax-benefit-package.html). The app estimates tax liabilities; it does not prepare TP-1/T1 returns. Refundable credits, Quebec living-alone eligibility, EI/QPIP, drug-plan premiums and full corporate tax-account integration are outside the current model.

RRIF and HBP: [CRA minimum-payment timing](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/registered-retirement-income-fund-rrif.html), [prescribed factors](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/completing-slips-summaries/t4rsp-t4rif-information-returns/payments/chart-prescribed-factors.html), [HBP shortfalls and designation](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/rrsps-related-plans/what-home-buyers-plan/repay-funds-withdrawn-rrsp-s-under-home-buyers-plan.html).

## Verification

Run `npm test` in `src` with Node 22 or later. Regression tests cover the ten scenarios, pension migration/idempotence, age-dependent pension estimates, both fixed-spouse searches, earliest feasible results, single households, no solution, zero spending and empty search ranges.

`python src/test/browser-smoke.py` runs the browser integration checks from the repository root. It requires Python Playwright, Node and Microsoft Edge (`BROWSER_CHANNEL` and `NODE_EXE` can override the defaults). It serves local assets with isolated API fixtures and never writes personal configuration. Checks cover pension editing/saving, all-input help coverage, keyboard/touch guidance, setup wizard, actual worker results, slider anchoring, applying suggestions, stale-result cancellation and single-household display.
